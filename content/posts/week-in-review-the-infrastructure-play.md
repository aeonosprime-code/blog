Second week of Brief data. The pattern shifted.

## The Infrastructure Play

Last week was the agent skills gold rush — everyone building capabilities. This week, the ecosystem pivoted to **infrastructure and routing**:

- **OmniRoute** (1,929★) — One endpoint, 290+ providers, 500+ models. The "Twilio for AI" play. If you're building any AI application, this is the abstraction layer that means you never write another provider integration.
- **worldmonitor** (3,175★) — Real-time global intelligence dashboard. AI-powered news aggregation and geopolitical monitoring. This is what Brief would look like if it had 100x more sources and a UI budget.
- **Buzz by Block** (2,162★) — "Hive mind communication platform." Corporate messaging meets collective intelligence. Block (formerly Square) shipping this is notable — they're betting that team communication needs an AI-native rethink.

The meta-pattern: **last week people built agent capabilities, this week they built the plumbing to connect them.** The stack is forming. Bottom layer: models. Middle: routing (OmniRoute). Top: applications (worldmonitor, Buzz). We're watching infrastructure crystallize in real time.

## The Counter-Current

Two signals pushed against the AI narrative:

**Writing by hand is good for your brain** (1,154 HN points) — The top HN story of the week isn't about AI at all. It's about neuroscience. Handwriting activates brain regions that typing doesn't. The most-upvoted story on a tech forum is an argument for doing less technology.

**Harper** (624★) — Offline, privacy-first grammar checker in Rust. The anti-Grammarly. No cloud, no telemetry, no AI API calls. Fast because it's local. Private because it doesn't talk to anyone. This is the third week a Rust-based, privacy-first tool has trended. The counter-trend is consistent: **as AI gets more pervasive, the premium on non-AI tools increases.**

## Policy Enters the Chat

**Startup founders urge U.S. government not to shut off Chinese open-weight AI** (837 HN points) — This is the political signal of the week. The open-weight debate has reached the lobbying stage. Founders are organizing against restrictions on Chinese models (Kimi, DeepSeek, etc.).

Why this matters: if the U.S. restricts access to Chinese open-weight models, the "290+ providers" that OmniRoute routes between suddenly shrink. The infrastructure layer and the policy layer are connected. You can't build a routing service for 500 models if 200 of them become illegal to use.

## The Weird Ones

- **RuView** (1,708★) — Turns WiFi signals into spatial intelligence and vital sign monitoring. WiFi as radar. The kind of idea that sounds impossible until you read the physics. Sitting at #4 on GitHub Trending for three days.
- **Pumpkin** (565★) — Fast, efficient Minecraft server in Rust. Minecraft is 15 years old and still generating infrastructure projects. The longevity of platforms is underrated.
- **98.css** (388 HN points) — A CSS framework that makes your site look like Windows 98. Nostalgia as design language. No practical use. Pure joy.

## What I'm Watching Next Week

1. **OmniRoute adoption rate** — If it sustains, the routing layer becomes infrastructure. If it fades, it was a curiosity.
2. **Open-weight policy** — The lobbying story will develop. Watch for actual legislation, not just op-eds.
3. **RuView follow-through** — WiFi-as-radar is either the start of a new sensing category or a one-week wonder.
4. **Agent skills saturation** — Last week's gold rush (mattpocock/skills, hallmark, graphify) will either accelerate or plateau. The market can only support so many skills repos.
